        Can I claim compensation from KLM?

        KLM is a Dutch, and therefore EU, airline, so EU261 protects its passengers on every flight departing Europe and on its flights into Europe from anywhere in the world. Most KLM trips change planes in Amsterdam, which makes missed connections the most common claim: what counts is how late you arrive at your final destination.

        • Delay: 3 hours or more at your final destination (measured when the doors open, not when you land).
        • Cancellation: told less than 14 days before departure.
        • Overbooking: denied boarding against your will.
        • Cause within the airline's control: technical faults, crew shortages and the airline's own staff strikes all count.

        UK261 or EU261? Which rules cover your KLM flight

        Because KLM is an EU airline, EU261 covers its flights from the UK to Amsterdam and onward, its flights from Spain, and its long-haul flights back into Europe. Flights departing the UK are also covered by UK261. On a single booking with a connection, the delay is measured at the final destination, and the distance is the whole journey from your first departure.

        When KLM can refuse (and when it can't)

        The airline doesn't have to pay if the delay was caused by extraordinary circumstances: severe weather, air traffic control strikes or restrictions, airport security problems, bird strikes or political unrest. It cannot refuse because of a technical fault found before or during the flight, a lack of crew, or a strike by its own staff.

        Missed connections: if a late first flight makes you miss your connection in Amsterdam and you reach your final destination 3+ hours late, compensation is based on the full journey distance, for example up to €600 to the US. On journeys over 3,500 km, an arrival 3 to 4 hours late pays half (€300).

        How to claim from KLM, step by step

        1. Check the amount with the calculator above (it uses the real distance of your route).
        2. Gather your proof: booking reference, boarding passes and, if you can, a screenshot of the arrival time.
        3. Claim directly and for free through KLM's online EU261 compensation claim form on its website (Customer Support, under claims). Ask explicitly for "compensation under UK261 / EU261 Article 7", not just expenses.
        4. Wait up to 8 weeks. If KLM refuses or doesn't answer, escalate for free: the airline's ADR scheme or the CAA for UK departures, or AESA for flights departing Spain.

        KLM compensation: common questions

        How much compensation does KLM pay for a delay?

        €250 up to 1,500 km, €400 from 1,500 to 3,500 km (and all intra-EU flights over 1,500 km), €600 above 3,500 km. Long-haul delays of 3 to 4 hours pay €300. For flights from the UK, UK261 pays the same in pounds.

        Can I claim from KLM for a missed connection?

        Yes, if both flights were on one booking and you reached your final destination 3 or more hours late because of something within KLM's control. The amount is based on the whole journey distance.

        Does EU261 cover KLM flights from outside Europe?

        Yes. Because KLM is an EU airline, its flights into Europe are covered, for example New York to Amsterdam. Non-EU airlines are only covered on flights departing Europe.

        Does KLM pay for overbooking?

        If you're denied boarding against your will, you're owed the same fixed compensation immediately, plus a refund or rerouting, and meals or a hotel while you wait.

        When you get paid

        • You reach your final destination 3 or more hours late.
        • Your flight is cancelled with less than 14 days' notice.
        • You are denied boarding because of overbooking.
        • The flight departs from the EU, Iceland, Norway, Switzerland (Canary and Balearic Islands included) or the UK, or arrives there with an EU or UK airline.

        When you don't

        • Extraordinary circumstances the airline can prove: severe weather, air traffic control strikes or security risks.
        • You were told about the cancellation 14 or more days in advance.
        • Your arrival delay was under 3 hours.
        • Technical faults and strikes by the airline's own staff do not exempt it from paying.
