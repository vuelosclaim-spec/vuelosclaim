Overbooked flight: what you're owed if you're denied boarding

If the airline sold more seats than it has and you're left behind against your will, compensation is automatic: extraordinary circumstances don't apply.

Work out my compensation

What you're owed

  • Compensation on the spot: £220 / €250 up to 1,500 km, £350 / €400 up to 3,500 km, £520 / €600 beyond, per passenger.
  • A refund or a new flight, your choice.
  • Care: food, drinks and a hotel if you have to wait until the next day.

The rules say compensation must be paid "immediately". Ask for it at the desk and, if they won't, claim afterwards with your boarding pass and written confirmation that you were denied boarding.

Conditions

  • A confirmed booking.
  • Checking in on time, or at least 45 minutes before departure if no time was given.
  • Not being refused for valid reasons (documents, safety, behaviour).

Should you volunteer?

Before bumping anyone, the airline must ask for volunteers. If you volunteer you get whatever you agree (cash, vouchers, points) plus a refund or new flight, but not the fixed compensation. Only worth it if the offer beats what you'd get if bumped, and cash is better than a voucher.

Downgraded?

If you're seated in a lower class than you paid for, you're owed 30%, 50% or 75% of that flight's price back depending on distance.

Common questions

Can the airline refuse overbooking compensation citing extraordinary circumstances?

No. Denied boarding due to overbooking is the airline's responsibility and compensation is automatic.

What happens if I volunteer?

You get what you agree with the airline plus a refund or new flight, but you give up the fixed £220–£520 / €250–€600.

How long do I have to claim?

Up to 6 years in England and Wales (5 in Scotland) and 5 years in Spain, but the sooner the better.
