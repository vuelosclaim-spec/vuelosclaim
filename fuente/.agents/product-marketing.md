# Product Marketing Context

*Last updated: 19 October 2026*

## Product Overview
**One-liner:** Free calculator that tells you in 10 seconds how much an airline owes you for a delayed, cancelled or overbooked flight to or from Spain.
**What it does:** The passenger enters origin, destination and airline. VuelosClaim computes the real great-circle distance and applies EU261 / UK261: €250/€400/€600 or £220/£350/£520 per passenger. Then it offers two routes: write the claim letter yourself for free, or hand it to a no-win-no-fee claims company (AirHelp, via affiliate).
**Product category:** Flight delay compensation calculator / "reclamar vuelo retrasado".
**Product type:** Free web tool + content site (static, bilingual ES/EN, 107+ pages: airlines, Spanish airports, guides).
**Business model:** Affiliate commission from claims companies (Travelpayouts → AirHelp, Compensair backup) + Google AdSense (pending approval). Free for users.

## Target Audience
**Primary segments:**
1. Spanish residents whose flight was delayed or cancelled (ES pages, Ryanair/Vueling/Iberia).
2. British holidaymakers flying UK ⇄ Spain/Canaries/Balearics with TUI, Jet2, easyJet, Ryanair (EN pages). This is where most Google impressions come from today.
3. British/European expats living in Spain.
**Primary use case:** "My flight was X hours late — do I get money, and how much?"
**Jobs to be done:**
- Know quickly and with certainty whether I'm owed money and how much.
- Claim it without paying 25–35% to a company (or, if I'm busy, hand it off without risk).
- Understand what to do when the airline refuses or ignores me.

## Problems & Pain Points
**Core problem:** Airlines don't tell passengers they're owed fixed compensation, and the rules (distance bands, 3h at arrival, extraordinary circumstances, UK261 vs EU261) are confusing.
**Why alternatives fall short:**
- Claims companies' calculators ask for email/booking first and push you to sign up and pay 25–35%.
- Airline forms only offer refunds or vouchers and hide the EU261 compensation form.
- Generic articles give ranges, not your amount for your route.
**What it costs them:** €250–€600 per passenger left unclaimed; hours on hold.
**Emotional tension:** Anger at the airline ("they treat us like cattle"), fear of being scammed by claims companies, feeling powerless.

## Competitive Landscape
**Direct:** AirHelp, Flightright, Reclamador, Skycop, AirAdvisor, Compensair — huge domain authority; their calculators are lead-capture funnels.
**Secondary:** Consumer orgs (OCU, Which?), AESA / CAA pages — authoritative but no calculator, dry.
**Indirect:** Airline's own claim form; doing nothing.

## Differentiation
- Instant amount with real route distance, no email, no signup.
- Honest "do it yourself for free" option with a ready-made letter, next to the paid option.
- Covers both EU261 (€) and UK261 (£) for UK ⇄ Spain trips — most sites cover only one.
- Airline- and airport-specific pages with worked route examples.

## Objections
| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| "Is this a scam / claims company?" | It's a free calculator; no data is stored; you can claim yourself with the free letter. |
| "The airline said it was extraordinary circumstances" | Technical faults, crew shortages and the airline's own staff strikes don't count; ask for the cause in writing. |
| "It was a package holiday" | Flight rights still apply. |
**Anti-persona:** Delays under 3h; non-EU airline flying into the EU; people wanting legal representation in court.

## Switching Dynamics
**Push:** Airline ignores claim / offers voucher; claims companies take a big cut.
**Pull:** Exact amount in 10 seconds, free letter.
**Habit:** Doing nothing ("they never pay").
**Anxiety:** "Will this cost me?", "Is my data safe?"

## Brand Voice
**Tone:** Helpful friend who knows the rules. **Style:** Direct, short sentences, concrete numbers. **Personality:** Honest, practical, on the passenger's side, calm.
