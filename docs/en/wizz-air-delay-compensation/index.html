        Can I claim compensation from Wizz Air?

        Wizz Air often reschedules flights by hours or moves them to another day, and many passengers accept the change without knowing a cash payment may be owed. If your Wizz Air flight arrived 3 hours or more late, was cancelled or significantly rescheduled less than 14 days before departure, or you were denied boarding, you can claim a fixed amount per passenger.

        • Delay: 3 hours or more at your final destination (measured when the doors open, not when you land).
        • Cancellation: told less than 14 days before departure.
        • Overbooking: denied boarding against your will.
        • Cause within the airline's control: technical faults, crew shortages and the airline's own staff strikes all count.

        UK261 or EU261? Which rules cover your Wizz Air flight

        Wizz Air flies under three airlines: Wizz Air Hungary and Wizz Air Malta, which are EU airlines, and Wizz Air UK. Flights departing Spain or any EU airport are covered by EU261 in euros; flights departing the UK by UK261 in pounds. A flight into the EU operated by Wizz Air Hungary or Malta is covered by EU261 too.

        When Wizz Air can refuse (and when it can't)

        The airline doesn't have to pay if the delay was caused by extraordinary circumstances: severe weather, air traffic control strikes, ATC restrictions whose underlying cause was itself extraordinary (CJEU, C-108/26), airport security problems, bird strikes or political unrest. It cannot refuse because of a technical fault found before or during the flight, a lack of crew, or a strike by its own staff.

        Schedule changes: moving your flight by several hours with less than 14 days' notice counts as a cancellation under EU261, so compensation can apply even if you flew on the new time. Keep the email Wizz Air sent you about the change, and claim the compensation in cash rather than accepting WIZZ credit unless you want it.

        Claiming Wizz Air compensation: the overview

        1. Check the amount with the calculator above (it uses the real distance of your route).
        2. Gather your proof: booking reference, boarding passes and, if you can, a screenshot of the arrival time.
        3. Claim directly and for free through Wizz Air's online claim form on its website (Help Centre, under refunds and compensation). Ask explicitly for "compensation under UK261 / EU261 Article 7", not just expenses.
        4. Give the airline time to answer. If Wizz Air refuses or doesn't answer, escalate for free: after 8 weeks, the airline's ADR scheme or the CAA for UK departures; after a month, AESA for flights departing Spain.
        5. Prefer not to deal with it? A claims company does it for you on a no-win, no-fee basis (typically 25–35% plus VAT).

        Wizz Air compensation: common questions

        How much compensation does Wizz Air pay?

        €250 for flights up to 1,500 km, €400 from 1,500 to 3,500 km and €600 beyond (or £220/£350/£520 for flights from the UK), per passenger, if you arrive 3+ hours late or are cancelled at short notice.

        Wizz Air moved my flight. Can I claim?

        If they told you less than 14 days before departure and the new times are several hours different, it can count as a cancellation with compensation. You can also choose a full refund instead of the new flight.

        Do I have to accept WIZZ credit?

        No. Compensation and refunds under EU261 are owed in money. Credit is only an option if you agree to it.

        Wizz Air didn't answer my claim. What next?

        Escalate for free: for a flight departing Spain, to AESA after a month without a satisfactory answer; for a flight departing the UK, to the airline's ADR scheme or the CAA after 8 weeks.

        If Wizz Air won’t pay

        • Flight departing the UK: after 8 weeks, AviationADR (the dispute scheme it belongs to).
        • Flight departing Spain, or arriving in Spain from outside the EU with an EU airline: if Wizz Air refuses or hasn’t replied within a month, claim for free with AESA within a year of your claim to the airline; its decision binds the airline.
        • Flight departing another EU country: that country’s national enforcement body.

        Documents to keep

        • Boarding pass and booking confirmation.
        • Emails, texts or notices from Wizz Air about the delay or cancellation, with their dates.
        • The reason you were given, ideally in writing.
        • Receipts for food, transport or hotels you paid for.

        Time limits

        • England and Wales: 6 years through the courts (Dawson v Thomson, 2014).
        • Spain: 5 years to claim from the airline, according to AESA.
        • Elsewhere, national law decides (Court of Justice of the EU, Case C-139/11).

        What about a Wizz Air flight into the UK from outside Europe?
